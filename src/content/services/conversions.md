## Using the space you've already got

If you need another room, the answer is sometimes above your head or on the other side of the utility room door. A loft or garage conversion doesn't need new foundations and often doesn't need planning permission. That usually makes it quicker and cheaper than an extension. We'll tell you honestly which option suits your home, even if it's the smaller job.

## Loft conversions

The first thing we check is head height. We'll measure it at the first visit, because it decides what's possible. The second is the stairs. A loft room is only as good as the way up to it, so we'll work out where a new staircase can go without spoiling the floor below.

Depending on your roof, that might mean a simple conversion with rooflights, a dormer to add headroom, or a hip-to-gable change on a semi or end-of-terrace. Most of the work happens from scaffolding outside, so the house stays liveable until we open up the stairs near the end.

## Garage conversions

Garages make good home offices, playrooms, bedrooms and annexes for family. We insulate the floor, walls and roof, fill the door opening with a wall and window that match the house, and bring in heating and power. The aim is a room that feels like it was always part of the house.

One thing to check: on some newer estates, permitted development rights for garages were removed when the houses were built. We'll look into it for you before you spend anything.
